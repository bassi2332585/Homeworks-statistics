Exercise 1: Derivation of Welford's Recurrence Formulas

Online, numerically stable algorithm for computing sample mean, sum of squares (SS), and variance in a single pass.

1. Mathematical Proof for Mean & Sum of Squares

Let $ ar{x}_k$ be the mean of the first $k$ data points $x_1, \dots, x_k$. Knuth's recurrence for the mean is:

$$ ar{x}_k = ar{x}_{k-1} + rac{x_k - ar{x}_{k-1}}{k} $$

Define the sum of squared differences $M_k = SS_k = \sum_{i=1}^k (x_i - ar{x}_k)^2$. To express $M_k$ in terms of $M_{k-1}$:

$$ M_k - M_{k-1} = \sum_{i=1}^k (x_i - ar{x}_k)^2 - \sum_{i=1}^{k-1} (x_i - ar{x}_{k-1})^2 $$ $$ = (x_k - ar{x}_k)^2 + \sum_{i=1}^{k-1} \left[ (x_i - ar{x}_k)^2 - (x_i - ar{x}_{k-1})^2 ight] $$

Using the identity $(a-b)^2 - (a-c)^2 = (c-b)(2a - b - c)$ with $b= ar{x}_k$ and $c= ar{x}_{k-1}$:

$$ M_k = M_{k-1} + (x_k - ar{x}_{k-1})(x_k - ar{x}_k) $$

Sample variance $s_k^2$ (with Bessel's correction $k-1$) and population variance $\sigma_k^2$ are then given by:

$$ s_k^2 = rac{M_k}{k-1} \quad ext{for } k \ge 2, \qquad \sigma_k^2 = rac{M_k}{k} $$

2. Concise Code Implementation (Core 3 Lines)

The core algorithm requires updating three state variables per data point $x_k$ at step $k$:

// Core 3-line update logic per item x at step k
const delta = x - mean;
mean += delta / k;
M += delta * (x - mean); // M = SS_k
// Sample variance: s2 = M / (k - 1)

4. Statistical Inference & Theoretical Answers

Formal answers to homework questions, unbiasedness proofs, and cybersecurity applications.

1. Comparison of Averages & Unbiasedness

When we compute the grand average of sample means $\overline{ ar{X}} = rac{1}{m} \sum_{j=1}^m ar{X}_j$ and the grand average of sample variances $\overline{s^2} = rac{1}{m} \sum_{j=1}^m s_j^2$, we observe:

$$ \lim_{m o \infty} \overline{ ar{X}} = \mu, \qquad \lim_{m o \infty} \overline{s^2} = \sigma^2 $$

Key Conclusion: Both the sample mean $ ar{X}$ and the sample variance $s^2$ (with Bessel's correction $n-1$) are unbiased estimators of the true population parameters $\mu$ and $\sigma^2$:

$$ E[ ar{X}] = \mu, \qquad E[s^2] = \sigma^2 $$

2. Why Results Are Useful in Inferential Statistics

In real-world cybersecurity, an analyst rarely knows the true theoretical distribution parameters ($\mu, \sigma^2$) of all possible penetration attempts across the globe.

  • Point Estimation: Unbiasedness guarantees that our empirical estimates do not systematically overestimate or underestimate security risk.
  • Bessel's Correction ($n-1$): Dividing by $n-1$ instead of $n$ accounts for the degree of freedom lost when estimating the mean from the sample itself.
  • Confidence Intervals: Knowing that $Var( ar{X}) = rac{\sigma^2}{n}$ allows computing exact margin of errors: $ ar{X} \pm z \cdot rac{s}{\sqrt{n}}$.

3. Impact of Increasing $n$ (Systems Attempted per Hacker)

As $n$ increases, two fundamental statistical phenomena occur:

Shrinking Dispersion of Sample Means: The variance of sample means decreases at rate $\mathcal{O}(1/n)$: $Var( ar{X}) = rac{\sigma^2}{n}$. Individual hacker average scores cluster tightly around $\mu$.
Shrinking Dispersion of Sample Variances: The variance of $s^2$ decays proportionally to $ rac{1}{n-1}$. For Gaussian data, $Var(s^2) = rac{2\sigma^4}{n-1}$. As $n$ grows, every single hacker's measured variance $s_j^2$ becomes an extremely precise estimate of $\sigma^2$.
